# Distribution Plan — Draw & Guess

Goal: rooms created by real strangers, not just friends. Success is a game night where you don't personally know half the room.

Core insight: **nobody discovers a party game alone.** Every tactic below is really "make it trivial for one person to summon a group."

---

## Gate: don't post until these are true

Posting before this burns your one first impression.

- [ ] Public URL works on iOS Safari and Android Chrome (S08)
- [ ] Join flow is under 15 seconds with no signup (S05)
- [ ] Solo mode exists so a lone visitor gets something (S14)
- [ ] Reconnection works — a locked phone doesn't kill the game (S11)
- [ ] One full round can be watched without explanation, from 3m away (S07)

---

## Phase D1 — Friends and the first hosted night (after S08, parallel to Phase 4)

The point here is **bugs and evidence**, not traffic.

- [ ] Pick a date: a Friday or Saturday, ~8pm ET. Games need to be bored and together.
- [ ] Make a Discord event / group text with the link. Say what it is in one sentence.
- [ ] Run 2–3 full games. Record it (OBS screen capture of the host screen + a phone camera angle if possible).
- [ ] Note where people got confused — that's your M3 backlog, in order.
- [ ] Screenshot the scoreboard. Every future post needs a real image.
- [ ] Second night two weeks later, with the fixes shipped. Two nights beats one.

**Deliverable:** a 60s recording of real people having fun. This asset is worth more than any cold link drop.

## Phase D3 — Community posts (one shot each, no spamming)

Rules: post as a person with a story. Never say "check out my app." One post per venue, ever. Answer every comment.

Priority order:

1. **r/InternetIsBeautiful** — highest ceiling, strict rules: no self-promo language, describe the thing playfully, title must be a hook. Post when solo mode works so a lone clicker gets value.
2. **r/WebGames** — purpose-built for this. Allowed to be proud. Include the playable link in the first line.
3. **r/playmygame** — small but real feedback, and it's the etiquette-correct place for a launch.
4. **Show HN** — title as a claim, not a product name. e.g. "I built a Jackbox-style drawing game that fixes the unfair timer problem". Post Tuesday–Thursday morning ET.
5. **Discord servers** — small streamers, university CS/gaming servers. Ask a mod first. These convert best because a channel is already a group.
6. **Bluesky / X with a video** — the 60s clip does the work, not the text.

Skip entirely: paid ads, Product Hunt (wrong audience for party games), TikTok/Reels (full-time job to compete), public lobby to attract randoms (buys moderation work, not users).

---

## Phase D4 — The compounding move

The thing that actually generates ongoing traffic:

- [ ] Reach out to **teachers and trivia/game-night hosts** — anyone who routinely has 8 bored people and a projector. One willing host replays the game weekly.
- [ ] Make a **reusable room link** or "host again" shortcut so recurring hosts don't rebuild the room each time
- [ ] Consider a "season" framing: rotate the prompt pack monthly, so returning hosts get new content

This is the only channel where one relationship produces repeated sessions.

---

## Metrics to watch (from S15)

- **rooms created** — the top of funnel
- **% of rooms with a completed round** — the lobby experience, the thing most likely to fail
- **rounds completed per room** — did anyone come back after round 1
- **time from click to first round** — the friction number

The failure mode to hunt: a room where one person joins and no round ever starts. Every UX improvement in the lobby is aimed at that.

## Blog post fodder to capture as it happens

Don't reconstruct these later:
- first hosted night: how many joined, how many finished, where it broke
- the first time a stranger played
- the r/InternetIsBeautiful comments (positive and hostile, both are quotable)
- rooms created vs. rounds completed after each post
